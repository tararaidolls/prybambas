Allergen plan

Current advice (AAP, NHS): don't delay common allergens. From around 6 months, introduce them one at a time, build up the amount, then keep them in the diet.

The 3-day rhythm
Day 1: introduce one allergen (tiny taste), at home, earlier in the day, when baby is well · Day 2: no new foods, only familiar ones · Day 3: repeat the allergen, a little more. Build up to a normal serving, then offer it a few times a week. Severe eczema or a known allergy? Ask your doctor first. The home page plans this for you.
Severe reaction: call emergency services
Trouble breathing, wheeze, swelling of tongue or throat, persistent cough, pale and floppy.
Mild reaction (hives, rash around the mouth, a few vomits): stop that food and call your doctor.

    Weaning guide

    Every baby moves at their own pace. Ages are a guide, not a deadline.

    Ready for solids?

    Most babies are ready around 6 months, and not before 4 months. Look for all three signs together:

    • Sits up with little or no support and holds the head steady.
    • Coordinates: looks at food, reaches for it and brings it to the mouth.
    • Swallows food instead of pushing it straight back out.

    Not signs of readiness: chewing fists, waking more at night, or wanting extra milk.

    Safe-eating basics
    • Always seat baby upright in a high chair and stay with them for the whole meal.
    • Never feed in a car seat, stroller, or while baby is lying back.
    • First finger foods should squash easily between your thumb and finger.
    • Cut round foods (grapes, cherry tomatoes, blueberries) into quarters lengthwise.
    • Let baby set the pace. Never force food.
    Gagging vs. choking
    Gagging: normal
    Noisy, coughing, red face, tongue pushing forward. Baby is learning. Stay calm.
    Choking: act now
    Silent, can't cry or cough, struggling to breathe, lips turning blue. Start infant first aid and call emergency services.
