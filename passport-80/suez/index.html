Stage 2 · Europe and Suez

The canal from above

How do giant ships get through a narrow canal in the desert? Watch the convoys.

Good to know

No locks. The two seas are at almost the same height, so the canal has no gates or steps. Water simply fills it from end to end.

Ships move in convoys. Much of the canal is too narrow for two big ships to pass. So a line of ships goes south while another line waits in the Great Bitter Lake, then they swap.

About 120 miles long. A trip through takes about half a day today.

Brand new in 1872. The canal opened in November 1869 — less than three years before Fogg sailed through it. Without it, his bet would have been impossible.

How many miles shorter is the canal route?

12,300 − 7,200 = 5,100 miles shorter.

How many days does a steamship need for each route?

Canal: 7,200 ÷ 300 = 24 days. Around Africa: 12,300 ÷ 300 = 41 days.

How many days does the canal save?

41 − 24 = 17 days.

Could Fogg have done it without the canal?

Almost certainly not. 17 extra days would take him far past 80 days — and he arrived home with only seconds to spare!
