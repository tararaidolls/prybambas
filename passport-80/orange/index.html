Why does this happen?

Every time Fogg moves a little east, he meets the sunrise a little earlier — about 4 minutes earlier for every degree. After going all the way around (360 degrees), those minutes add up to 24 hours. So Fogg saw one more sunrise than his friends in London.

That is why Fogg's own calendar ran one day ahead. He arrived in London thinking he was five minutes too late — until Passepartout found out it was still Saturday. He had made it after all!

Navigator 9–12 And if he had travelled west?

Then he would have “run away” from the sun and seen one sunrise fewer: 79 instead of 80. Today the International Date Line in the Pacific fixes this: crossing it eastward, you repeat a day.
